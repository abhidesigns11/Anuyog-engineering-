What "stainless steel" actually means on your order

The phrase covers a lot of ground, and the difference between two quotations for the same item is usually hidden in the grade, the sheet thickness and the finish. Here is exactly how we specify a job, so you can compare like with like.

Material grades

We work mainly in three grades, and we state which one on every quotation:

  • SS 304 — the default for furniture, lockers, dustbins, trolleys and general utility items. Good corrosion resistance in normal plant conditions.
  • SS 316 — where chlorides are present: coastal sites, saline solutions, wash-down with hard water, most food brine contact.
  • SS 316L — the low-carbon variant, for product-contact surfaces in pharmaceutical vessels where weld-zone corrosion is a concern.

Mild steel is used only for structures that are painted or clad and never touch product — stands, frames, external supports. We say so plainly if any part of a job is MS.

Material comes from mill-approved stockists in Mumbai and Ahmedabad, and we can supply the mill test certificate with the delivery when you ask for it at the order stage. Ask afterwards and we can usually still trace it, but not always.

Sheet thickness

This is where cheap quotations hide. A locker body in 0.8 mm sheet and one in 1.2 mm look identical in a photograph and behave very differently after two years of doors slamming. Our standard thicknesses are 1.2 mm for locker and cabinet bodies, 1.5 mm for seats, tops and working surfaces, and 2 mm and above for tank shells depending on diameter and duty. Every quotation states the thickness. If you have received a cheaper number elsewhere, that is the first line to compare.

Surface finish

  • Matt buff — the standard finish on furniture and utility items. Hides handling marks, wipes clean.
  • Mirror polish — for visible surfaces and for internal surfaces of vessels where residue must not key into the grain.

Internal welds on product-contact equipment are ground and polished to a continuous radius. There is no such thing as a cleanable sharp internal corner, so we do not leave any.

Fabrication and welding

Product-contact welds are TIG with argon backing purge on pipe and shell work. Structural and non-contact welds may be MIG. Welders working on pharmaceutical vessels work to a documented procedure and their work is checked by dye penetrant test on request.

Stainless work is physically separated from carbon steel work in the shop — separate benches, separate grinding media, separate tooling. Iron pick-up is invisible on the day of dispatch and shows up as rust spotting in a customer's plant a month later, which is a problem nobody wants to be arguing about.

Testing and documentation

Depending on the item, the following can be supplied with the delivery. Tell us at the order stage which you need, because some of them have to be planned into the build:

  • Mill test certificate for the material
  • Dye penetrant test report on welds
  • Hydrostatic test report for tanks, vessels and jackets (shell and jacket tested separately)
  • Surface finish record
  • Dimensional inspection report against the approved drawing
  • General arrangement drawing, as-built

How an order runs

An inquiry becomes a quotation, usually within one working day. If the item is custom, we may come back with questions or ask for a site visit before pricing — for a wall-run of cabinets or a tank that has to go through an existing doorway, a measurement is worth more than a phone call. On confirmation we issue a general arrangement drawing for your approval, and fabrication starts only after that drawing comes back signed. Standard lead time is two to four weeks from approved drawing; large vessels run longer and we say so up front rather than after you have placed the order.

Delivery is by road across Gujarat, Daman and Maharashtra, and by transporter beyond that. Our fitters handle positioning and on-site assembly for benches, locker banks, cabinet runs and tanks that ship in sections.

Warranty

Twelve months from delivery against fabrication defects — weld failure, joint separation, hardware failure under normal use. Not covered: corrosion from a chemical the equipment was not specified for, damage in transit where the customer arranged transport, modifications made by others, and normal wear on moving parts such as castors and hinges. If something fails inside the warranty we repair or replace it; we do not argue about it over email for a month first.
